1. Terms you must be able to define on the spot

If an interviewer reads your CV, these are the words most likely to get a direct "what does that mean?" follow-up.

Docs-as-code — writing and managing documentation the way developers manage code: plain-text files (usually Markdown), stored in Git, reviewed via pull requests, and published through an automated build/CI pipeline.
DITA (Darwin Information Typing Architecture) — an XML standard for structured authoring, built around small reusable "topics" instead of long documents.
DITA topic types — Concept (explains an idea), Task (step-by-step instructions), Reference (tables, specs, parameters).
DITA map — the file that assembles individual topics into a structured, ordered output (like a table of contents that also controls what gets published).
Single-sourcing / content reuse — writing content once and publishing it across multiple outputs (PDF, web, print) or multiple products without duplicating it.
Structured authoring — enforcing a defined content model/format (like DITA) rather than free-form writing, so content is consistent and machine-processable.
SOP (Standard Operating Procedure) — a documented, repeatable process that standardizes how a task is performed.
Content/documentation governance — the rules, ownership, and review processes that keep documentation accurate, consistent, and up to date over time.
Style guide compliance — writing to a defined voice/tone/formatting standard (e.g., the Google Developer Documentation Style Guide) rather than personal preference.
Content lifecycle — the stages content moves through: creation → review → publish → maintenance → retirement.

3. Interview questions by CV section

Each line is a likely question with a short cue on what your answer should hit — write your own real answer underneath each one before an interview.

Career background & transition

You have a Biotechnology degree — what took you into content/technical writing?Cover: the genuine reason, and one transferable skill (precision, structured thinking) from your science background.
You moved from SEO content writing into technical documentation — what carried over?Cover: audience-first thinking, clarity, structuring content for scanning/search.
Walk me through your career progression.Cover: what actually changed in scope/ownership at each step — be specific, not just chronological.

Core role at Google (via Cognizant)

Describe the end-to-end documentation lifecycle for an SV hardware/imagery topic.Cover: SME input → drafting → review → publish → maintenance, in your own words.
How do you decide web help article vs. user guide vs. product spec sheet?Cover: audience and use-case differences for each format.
What do you do differently for content that will be translated?Cover: concise sentences, avoiding idioms, consistent terminology.
Tell me about a bottleneck or content gap you found and fixed.Cover: a real, specific example with a before/after.
What does the Google style guide require beyond a generic style guide?Cover: specific voice/tone/formatting rules you actually apply.

Documentation quality & governance

Walk me through your documentation quality-scoring framework.Cover: what you measured, how scores were calculated, who used them.
What changed after you introduced scoring?Cover: a concrete behavior or output change, even if not numeric.
What SOPs did you write, and what problem existed before them?Cover: the "before" state and what the SOP standardized.
How do you handle disagreement during content review?Cover: a real example of resolving a review disagreement professionally.

Team coordination & training

How did you decide task allocation across the KM team?Cover: criteria you used — skill fit, workload, deadlines.
What does onboarding a new writer look like from your side?Cover: concrete steps — tools, style guide walkthrough, shadowing, first assignment.
What's your actual scope of authority — is this a formal lead role?Cover: be precise and honest — task coordination is real, but say plainly if you don't have formal reports.

The 4.45/5 client feedback score

What does that score measure, and who gives it?Cover: source and scope of the feedback — be specific and accurate.
What feedback pulled the score down, and what changed?Cover: one real example of acting on feedback, if you have one.

Tools & technical skills

Which CMS have you used day-to-day? Likes/dislikes?Cover: real, specific experience with Google's CMS.
What's your actual hands-on comfort with Git and Markdown?Cover: be honest about current level — see Section 2 to close gaps before interviews.
Have you worked hands-on in DITA/XML, or is it conceptual knowledge?Cover: be honest — "conceptual understanding, no production experience yet" is a fine answer if true.

Earlier SEO/content roles

How did writing for traffic/conversions shape how you write now?Cover: audience-first writing, scannability, clarity under attention constraints.
You moderated user-generated content — what judgment calls did that involve?Cover: real examples of compliance/quality decisions you made.

Behavioral / scenario

A stakeholder wants faster publishing than your quality process allows. What do you do?Cover: how you'd balance speed and quality — a real or realistic approach.
You inherit a messy, ownerless documentation set. Where do you start?Cover: audit → prioritize → assign ownership → establish a maintenance cadence.
How would you introduce a quality-scoring system to a team that's never had one?Cover: buy-in first, pilot small, iterate based on team feedback.

4. Honest gaps — prepare these answers deliberately

DITA/XML hands-on experience. You have conceptual knowledge, not production experience. Don't oversell it — say plainly: "I understand structured authoring principles and DITA's topic model; I haven't worked in a live DITA environment yet, but I've prepared for it." This is a normal, credible answer.
"Technical Documentation Lead" vs. formal title "Technical Writer." If asked whether you have people-management responsibility, answer honestly: task coordination and training, not formal direct reports. Don't let this rattle you — it's a common and respected scope of responsibility even without the "manager" label.
No AI-assisted documentation, RAG, or enterprise search experience. If asked, it's fine to say this is an area you're aware of and interested in but haven't worked on directly yet — don't claim it.

5. Two-week prep checklist

  • Read Markdown Guide basic syntax page (30 min)
  • Complete GitHub's "Hello World" Git tutorial (30 min)
  • Read Write the Docs' docs-as-code overview (30 min)
  • Read DITA Open Toolkit's "Introduction to DITA" (45 min)
  • Skim one Scriptorium blog post on single-sourcing/content reuse
  • Read the Google Developer Documentation Style Guide overview page
  • Write your own 2–3 sentence answer for every question in Section 3
  • Rehearse honest answers for the three gaps in Section 4 out loud
  • Reread your CV once, out loud, and make sure you can defend every single line
